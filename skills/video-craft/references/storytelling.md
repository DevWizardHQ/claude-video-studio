# Storytelling for tutorials and marketing

## Structure that works
Hook -> promise -> steps with one snag -> result -> next action.

- **Hook (0-5s):** open on the viewer's problem or the finished result, never the logo.
- **Promise (5-10s):** one sentence that says what they will be able to do.
- **Steps:** at most 7 numbered beats. If there are more, split the video.
- **Snag:** show one realistic failure and its recovery. It builds trust and prevents support calls.
- **Result:** hold the success screen long enough to read (3s).
- **Next action:** one thing to do, one place to get help.

## Rules
- Tension in a tutorial is "will it work?". Resolve it quickly and visibly.
- Name the viewer's feeling once ("code ashche na, tension nebon na") and move on.
- Use the viewer's words, not product internals ("account kholun", not "user provisioning").
- Order by the viewer's journey, not by the menu structure.
- End with a concrete action and the support contact, never with "thanks for watching" alone.

## Marketing variant
Problem -> proof -> product moment -> benefit -> call to action. 30 to 45 seconds for feeds. One claim, one proof.

## Checks
- Can the promise be said in one sentence?
- Does every scene move the viewer closer to the promise?
- Is there a scene that exists only because the screen exists? Cut it.
